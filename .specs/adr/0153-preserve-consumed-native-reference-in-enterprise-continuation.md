# Preserve the consumed native reference in Enterprise continuation

Date: 2026-09-11

Status: Accepted for the prospective continuation. Native execution still
requires the existing operational custody, registered design and current
admission checks.

## Context

[E13 completed one Turso reference](../../evaluations/reports/evolution/e13/README.md)
before its controller stopped. [ADR 0150](0150-continue-enterprise-searches-by-family-readiness.md)
requires twelve starting roles and preserves finite inherited search budgets.
[ADR 0152](0152-match-pinned-model-cache-exceptions-to-execution-phases.md)
corrects the specific cache declaration that interrupted execution. Continuing
must preserve the completed observation without charging or executing it again.

The completed job used the exact public task and full reference package needed
by the successor. Harbor's pinned native analyzer supports the original job and
its original evaluated skill path. A same-byte package at another path does
not establish that original evaluation's provenance.

## Decision

Declare exactly one original starting import: E13, Turso, generation zero,
candidate `baseline`. Bind its original allocation, job, native lock, result,
staged package, reconciliation and closed predecessor ledger. Keep the original
job directory, evaluated skill path, logical name and observation unchanged.
Use the native owner's canonical analysis without constructing a replacement
job, synthetic evaluated-skill override or renamed predecessor archive.

The successor keeps all twelve starting roles: one consumed original and eleven
new measurements. Generation-zero dispatch excludes the imported baseline.
Every later archive keeps that baseline's original identity. The owner must
validate its provenance and comparability with current results before the
family qualifies or selects a candidate.

Seed the shared allocation counter with the consumed identity. Development
retains its cumulative cap of 387, with at most 386 new allocations. Turso
retains 56 cumulative allocations, with at most 55 new allocations. Proposal
claims remain 91 of 585, with at most 373 further claims in the five open
families. Pending identities, closed catalogs, miss and round counters, and
Entity's exclusive first measurement retain their existing contracts.

Report original, new and cumulative trial counts separately. Reject an import
counted as new, a different original score or source path, and any additional
undeclared import. All eight family obligations, the joint development replay,
the all-500 comparison and the one-way whole-bundle acceptance remain required.
No private feedback, partial promotion or budget reset is introduced.

## Verified evidence and limits

The pinned Harbor 0.18.0 loader accepted the original with canonical analysis,
locked provenance, integrity qualification and the original mean reward
`0.4542900785976876`. The full 252-file package matched its fixed reference and
the current public template matched the original job signature. Negative checks
rejected a same-byte source under a different path and a changed job signature.
The first software probe preserved an assertion-wording error at an expected
rejection; its corrected successor passed. Neither probe executed a native job
or generated a new quality observation.

All 89 runtime tests passed on pinned Linux Harbor. Sixteen reporting tests
passed, including original-versus-new accounting. All nineteen declared native
job profiles passed the existing dry-run and doctor preflight with zero trial
dispatches. Application coverage passed at 90.5% with 1,636 tests and 373
subtests. These software checks do not prove complete family qualification,
retrieval improvement or final acceptance.

The [safe readiness aggregate](../../evaluations/reports/evolution/e13/turso-baseline-and-controller-stop-001/continuation-readiness.json)
binds the original proof and preflight receipts. The original E13 study remains
stopped. The operational integration and registered-lock review are subsequent
steps; this decision does not attest them retroactively.
